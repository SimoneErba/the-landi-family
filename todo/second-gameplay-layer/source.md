I think The House needs a second gameplay layer. Right now, you have a family simulation, but not enough of a game.

You can observe people, inspect their personalities, and watch time pass. But you're missing three things:

1. Agency: meaningful actions the player can initiate.
2. Goals: something to work toward over several months or years.
3. Conflict: obstacles, competition, risks, and difficult choices.

I would borrow heavily from Crusader Kings III and Norland, but adapt their mechanics to a rural Italian family in 1850.

And I particularly like your idea of adding a map. However, I'd start with a village map, not a map of Italy.

## 3. Make village families into competing actors

This could be the mechanic that transforms the game.

Instead of managing one isolated household, simulate perhaps 8–15 important families in the village.

Each has property, wealth, relationships, ambitions, and a history.

They should act independently of the player.

## 5. Give the player long-term ambitions

Without long-term goals, adding character actions will make the game busier but might not actually make it fun.

Some possible ambitions:

| Ambition              | Gameplay                                                         |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Rise from poverty     | Build wealth, acquire land, develop businesses                   |
| Become respected      | Build relationships, gain reputation, obtain village positions   |
| Build a dynasty       | Arrange marriages, secure inheritance, maintain family influence |
| Produce a scholar     | Educate a child, fund university, build intellectual connections |
| Escape the village    | Save money, develop urban contacts, migrate                      |
| Preserve family unity | Resolve feuds, prevent estrangement, manage succession           |

These should be player-selected aspirations, not necessarily rigid victory conditions. You can change goals as the family evolves.

## 7. What I would implement first

I wouldn't build the map first. A map by itself will not fix the underlying problem.

I'd implement the following in order:

Phase 1 — Character actions

Implement 10–15 long-running activities, with duration, cost, prerequisites, progress, interruption and outcomes. Actions can be performed autonomously or assigned by the player.

Phase 2 — Other families

Add 5–10 NPC households with relationships, professions, property, ambitions and autonomous actions. This creates a social environment for your mechanics.

Phase 3 — Village map

Introduce clickable properties and important buildings. Initially it can simply be a static illustrated map with interaction points, not an animated world.

Phase 4 — Ownership and power

Introduce buying land, businesses, social standing, political offices, loans and inheritance disputes. This gives the player medium- and long-term objectives.

Phase 5 — Italy map

Expand the playable world with city-to-city travel, education, trade, employment and migration. Only do this once the village works as a game.

There's one distinction I would preserve from Norland: its characters have their own will, and actions are conditional on traits, relationships, resources and circumstances. Its official action system already uses those principles.&#x20;

## One big design decision remains

Do you want The House to primarily be a game about:

A. Family and psychology — Relationships, marriage, secrets, education, ambitions, tragedies. Closer to CK3's character simulation.

B. Village economy and power — Acquire land, manage businesses, compete with other households, become influential. Closer to Norland.

C. Both, tightly integrated — Economic ambitions create family drama, while psychological traits influence economic decisions.

Develop this direction&#x20;

My preference is C, but with a crucial constraint: the economy should exist to create personal dilemmas, rather than becoming a separate factory-management game.

A father might want to expand the family farm. His son wants to become a doctor. His daughter wants to marry into a rival family. The family has enough money to pursue only one of these ambitions.

That's where I think The House could become genuinely interesting.
