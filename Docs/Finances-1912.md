# Italian household finances around 1912

Historical background from the earlier scenario. The current game begins in rural Tuscany in 1800; these twentieth-century benchmarks do not establish 1800 wages or food costs. See [the current currency and history notes](Tuscany-History.md).

## Evidence

Paolo Raspadori, *Becoming Workers: Strikes by Hotel and Restaurant Staff in Italy (1902–1923)*, International Review of Social History (2015), p. 386, footnote 17, cites Vera Zamagni's industrial wage series: an average monthly factory wage of **73.84 lire in 1913**. This is a nearby-year benchmark, not an exact 1912 wage for every occupation or region. The article also documents large differences between jobs and payment arrangements.

Source: https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/C407B91AAF88D7FAD7615769BCB7F98E/S0020859015000528a.pdf/becoming-workers-strikes-by-hotel-and-restaurant-staff-in-italy-19021923.pdf

Giovanni Federico, Alessandro Nuvolari, and Michelangelo Vasta, *The Origins of the Italian Regional Divide: Evidence from Real Wages, 1861–1913*, Journal of Economic History (2019), pp. 68–71, construct subsistence baskets from provincial prices. Their basket supplies 1,940 calories per consumption unit and includes minimal clothing, fuel, soap, and lighting. Four family members are represented as three consumption units, with rent added at 5%, giving 3.15 baskets. Regional baskets differ, including maize versus wheat bread and butter versus olive oil. They use about 250 working days/year for international comparisons. These assumptions show why a universal food allowance is an abstraction; the study does not establish 10 lire/person/month as a national 1912 food bill.

Source: https://www.iris.sssup.it/retrieve/dd9e0b32-40fd-709e-e053-3705fe0a83fd/FNV_real_wages_Italy_JEH.pdf

## Prototype choices

The player requested January 1912, 100 lire in savings, 10 lire per resident per month for food, and monthly inflation of 2% / 12. Those are gameplay settings, not a reconstruction of actual inflation or total historical expenses. Monthly compounding yields about 2.0184% growth over twelve months. Food rounds per person to the nearest centesimo before multiplying by residents. The second monthly food bill is 10.02 lire, so a single non-earner's balance goes 100 → 90 → 79.98.

Estimated resident monthly earnings, guided by the nearby industrial benchmark:

| Resident | Occupation | Lire/month |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Giovanni | Shopkeeper, net earnings | 80 |
| Maria | Household manager, unpaid | 0 |
| Carlo | Shop worker | 70 |
| Anna | Student | 0 |
| Pietro | Carpenter | 85 |
| Lucia | Seamstress | 35 |
| Sofia | Apprentice seamstress | 15 |

These individual amounts are design estimates, not observed historical wages for those exact occupations. Giovanni's net shop income and Carlo's pay are treated as separate contributions, so the shopkeeper amount is after worker pay and operating costs. A business accounting system is still pending. All resident earnings currently enter one shared purse; independent branch budgets and negotiated contributions remain future work.

Only food is charged for now. Rent, fuel, clothing, upkeep, taxes, education, and medical costs need later systems. The resulting surplus is therefore intentionally generous and should not be interpreted as realistic disposable income. Salaries remain nominally fixed while food prices rise. Negative balances retain unpaid bills rather than silently clamping the purse to zero.
