# Wages, education and apprenticeships around 1800

Research audit, 12 October 2026. The game starts in rural Tuscany, so Florence urban payrolls are benchmarks, not a complete rural salary schedule. This pass records evidence and implementation implications; it does not change wages or tuition.

## Wage evidence

Ridolfi and Vasta, Reffici debet! The real wages of the workers of the Dome of Florence (1326-1861), University of Siena working paper 928 (July 2025), uses cathedral payrolls and other archives. Figure 3 (printed p.14, PDF page 15) visually indicates roughly **4-5 grams of silver per workday for building laborers** and **6-7 for building craftsmen around 1800**. These are approximate chart readings, not exact annual observations. Figure 6 and the surrounding discussion show working years of roughly 259-284 days in 1750-1861 for the selected established-worker sample. Casual workers could have fewer paid days; the cathedral sample may be privileged. Board was uncommon in this urban construction sample. These results cannot establish farm, domestic-service, female sewing or apprentice pay.

Source: https://www.deps.unisi.it/sites/st02/files/allegatiparagrafo/23-07-2025/928.pdf

Using the game's existing conversion convention, 1 base Tuscan lira corresponds to 0.84 later Italian lire; at 4.5 g silver per Italian lira this gives 3.78 g per base lira. This is a **conversion assumption for calibration**, not verification of every circulating coin in 1800. Thus the chart values imply roughly 1.1-1.3 base lire per paid day for laborers and 1.6-1.9 for craftsmen. Assuming 260-280 paid days per year gives monthly equivalents of about **23-31** and **34-43** base lire respectively. Actual calendar-month earnings must follow paid days. A broader 25-35 / 35-50 range would be a design allowance, not a historical observation.

The commonly reused Malanima long-run dataset switches from Tuscany to Lombardy after 1605; its 1800 row must not be labeled Tuscan. Source: https://www.economics.utoronto.ca/munro5/MalanimaItalyPricesWages.pdf

A separate institutional anchor is the late-eighteenth-century Florence public-school teacher salary of **96 scudi**, recorded in the Tuscan Regional Council's publication (printed p.161, PDF page 165). The same volume gives the accounting conversion **1 scudo = 7 lire**. On an annual interpretation, consistent with the annual school expenditure context, this is 672 Tuscan lire/year, or 56/month averaged. It is a particular established teaching post, not every rural schoolmaster's pay.

Source: https://www.consiglio.regione.toscana.it/upload/eda/pubblicazioni/vol1.pdf

## Education costs

**Verified zero tuition, restricted access:** Florence's municipal archive states that the Leopoldine schools for poor girls were free under the 1783 regulation, teaching religion, reading, writing, arithmetic and textile work. Free tuition does not mean zero household cost: supplies, travel, admission restrictions and the loss of work/help must be modeled separately. This does not establish universal free schooling in every rural village.

Source: https://cultura.comune.fi.it/pagina/archivio-storico/patrimonio-documentario

**A local boarding benchmark:** Massimo Parri's study of Santa Chiara in San Gimignano reports the usual fee up to 1800 as **30 scudi annually**. At the accounting conversion above this is 210 Tuscan lire/year, or 17.5/month averaged. This is a boarding-institution example, not primary-school tuition or a universal secondary-school tariff. The provider's PDF currently returns a verification page; the figure and institutional scope were checked through indexed text and the journal's bibliographic index. Further checking of the complete fee inclusions is needed before implementation.

Sources: https://www.storicavaldelsa.it/sites/default/files/download/Parri-Educande.pdf and https://www.istitutodatini.it/biblio/riviste/u-z/valdel80.htm

**Unresolved:** I did not establish an exact rural elementary fee, private tutor price or Pisa/Siena university fee schedule specifically for 1800. A modern uniform primary/secondary/university price ladder would imply precision unsupported by these findings.

## Were apprentices paid?

There is direct Tuscan institutional evidence of paid apprenticeship. The State Archive of Prato documents masters working in the Magnolfi orphanage workshops, taking orphans as apprentices and paying wages deposited monthly in individual savings accounts. Institutional history places this arrangement in the later 1830s, rather than 1800. It confirms that the century-long game cannot classify every apprenticeship as unpaid classroom study. It does not establish a universal 1800 apprentice wage or a fixed percentage of a craftsman's pay.

Sources: https://archiviodistatoprato.cultura.gov.it/home/mostre/lungo-il-filo-dellaffido/22-la-formazione-professionale and https://www.metastasio.it/it/teatri

A historically defensible design should represent the terms of a particular placement: training, productive work, any cash remuneration, any board/lodging, and any payment to the master. Precise 1800 Tuscan contracts still need verification before assigning universal amounts. Do not use medieval Florentine guild contracts, nineteenth-century Prato orphanage wages or modern statutory apprenticeships interchangeably.

## Audit of the current game

- The catalog pays a carpenter 85 lire/month and a builder 65. The skilled Florence payroll benchmark above suggests these amounts are high for an ordinary employee around 1800. A shop owner's profits are a different quantity and should not be substituted for wage pay.
- The catalog's farm laborer earns 35/month, but the authored Carlo earns 70. Neither is currently backed by an identified 1800 rural payroll. Owner-family farming receipts also cannot be treated as an independent wage for every person helping on the same farm.
- Basic schooling charges 2/month, secondary study 8/month, and each trade apprenticeship charges 3/month. These are balance values, not documented school tariffs.
- Careers.apply_request sets income to zero for every training program, while the authored Sofia already earns 15/month as an apprentice. This inconsistency needs a distinct work-and-training placement model.
- The catalog permits broadly modern occupations and qualification ladders from 1800. Availability should depend on era, institution and locality, as well as skills.

Implementation implications: separate daily-paid work, established annual salary, family-enterprise receipts and in-kind provision; distinguish subsidized day schooling, private lessons and boarding; treat apprenticeships as contract-specific work/training, with compensation independent of tuition. Revise food and farm production alongside cash wages: cutting wages while keeping all food market-bought would create a misleading rural household budget. The previously modeled tax classes and imputed property yields remain estimates and also require recalibration against a coherent budget.
