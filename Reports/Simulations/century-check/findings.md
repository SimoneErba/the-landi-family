# Findings from the first policy-driven century batch

All 15 simulations completed 1,200 months: five player strategies, three common event seeds, the same starting family, and the same discount bonus and wealth ambition. This is 18,000 simulated family-months and 1,515 recorded yearly checkpoints. Requests used normal consent rules; no wealth, spouses or qualifications were granted by the runner.

Values above are medians across three seeds, not a single exemplary run. The full report includes ranges, individual runs and yearly histories.

## What this reveals

- Education performs best in this sample: median savings are about 69.5% above passive play. All three education runs finish with five university-educated and four influential family members. The first influential member appears in 1826–1835. Education could meet the influence criteria, even though this batch selected the wealth ambition.
- Business policies successfully purchase land, pay for buildings, ask managers and operate three productive properties. Their median savings are only about 5.6% above passive play, with average end-of-run stress around 0.60. Business effort and its family burden may deserve stronger rewards or more strategic uses later.
- Frugal employment suggestions do not consistently beat passive choices. End-of-run mean stress has a median of about 0.75. Conserving immediate money does not automatically produce the best lifetime family result.
- The family policy generates many actual refusals, so it does not simply force relatives to stay or marry. Marriage requests remain unfulfilled goals because partner generation is unfinished. Median savings are lower than passive play, while stress remains low.
- Family size never peaks above eight across this batch. Marriage, new households and further generations are the dominant progression gap; multiplying fertility alone would not fix it.
- No tested policy reaches the million-lire wealth ambition, and none ends a sampled month in debt. The starter family is financially forgiving, but the wealth objective requires substantially more progression than these implemented policies provide.
- People over 100 remain alive. Mortality and succession are incomplete, so this is a century-long execution of the current mechanics, not validation of a complete generational game.

The policies are deliberately simple, including eligibility-based career selection and repeated proposals at two-year intervals. Only three event seeds were tested per policy. These results identify promising changes and missing systems; they do not prove an optimal strategy or universal balance. No further gameplay values were changed in this task.
